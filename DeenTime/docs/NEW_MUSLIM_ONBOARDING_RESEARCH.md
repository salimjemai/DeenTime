# IqamaTime New Muslim Journey

**Research and product execution plan**  
**Prepared:** August 19, 2026  
**Status:** Proposed for product, scholar, safeguarding, and mosque review

> This document is a product research plan, not a religious ruling. All teaching material, translations, and school-specific guidance must be approved by qualified scholars and the participating mosque before publication.

## Executive decision

IqamaTime should not try to teach “everything about Islam” as one large course. The strongest model is a calm, personalized journey that gives a new Muslim the minimum needed to worship and feel supported immediately, then develops knowledge and practice over the first year and beyond.

The proposed product is **IqamaTime New Muslim Journey**: a mosque-managed learning and support program combining:

- a private learner portal;
- a first-72-hours checklist;
- a guided 30-, 90-, and 365-day curriculum;
- visual Salah and wudu instruction;
- Quran reading, recitation, and Arabic literacy;
- foundational Aqidah, Fiqh, Seerah, Hadith, and character;
- a named mentor and a route to a qualified scholar;
- local classes, mosque events, and practical referrals;
- multilingual, accessible lessons;
- mosque website widgets and authenticated APIs.

The product must be designed around four outcomes: **worship, understanding, belonging, and safety**.

## What the research shows

### 1. Sequence essentials instead of creating information overload

Established programs consistently begin with foundational beliefs and worship, then add daily life and deeper study. [New Muslim Academy](https://www.newmuslimacademy.org/our-courses/) separates introductory practical topics from advanced belief and worship courses. [NewMuslims.com](https://www.newmuslims.com/lessons) uses levels and categories spanning belief, worship, Quran, character, and social life. The [iERA Step-by-Step course](https://newmuslim.iera.org/wp-content/uploads/2021/01/STEP-BY-STEP_Student-Notes-Full-.pdf) uses a structured 13-week sequence rather than an encyclopedia.

**Product implication:** Show the right next step, not the entire library. Advanced material remains searchable but should not compete with the learner’s immediate priorities.

### 2. Human support is a core feature

The [New Muslim Foundation Guidance Hub](https://newmuslimfoundation.org/courses/) combines lessons with weekly gatherings, in-person contact, and mentors. [SeekersGuidance](https://seekersguidance.org/answers/calling-to-islam/what-are-some-recommendations-on-what-to-study-for-a-new-muslim/) similarly recommends study with teachers and community connection.

**Product implication:** Mentor assignment, check-ins, local classes, and scholar escalation are first-class workflows—not links added after the course is built.

### 3. Social transition can be as difficult as learning worship

The report [Between Isolation & Integration](https://www.convertmuslimfoundation.org.uk/UserUpload/filemanager/source/Research%20and%20Reports/Between%20Isolation%20%20Integration%20-%20LCC%20Report.pdf) documents isolation, family and friendship changes, cultural pressure, confusion, practical hardship, and lack of structured follow-up. [ISPU’s Reimagining Muslim Spaces](https://ispu.org/research-areas/reimagining-muslim-spaces/) includes converts among groups whose access and belonging mosque leaders should intentionally improve.

**Product implication:** Include family conversations, workplace practice, loneliness, culture versus religion, marriage boundaries, financial hardship, and local referrals. Do not reduce onboarding to videos about ritual.

### 4. Quran reading needs its own gradual path

The [New Muslims Circle IQRA program](https://www.newmuslimscircle.org/3078-2/) progresses from individual Arabic letters to Quran reading and tajweed. This is different from learning conversational Arabic or studying Quran meanings.

**Product implication:** Maintain three clearly separated but connected tracks:

1. Quran relationship and meaning;
2. Quran-script literacy and tajweed;
3. Arabic language and vocabulary.

### 5. Short practice and spaced review are more effective than binge learning

Reviews of learning research find broad benefits from spacing and retrieval practice. See the [Nature review of spacing and retrieval](https://doi.org/10.1038/s44159-022-00089-1) and the [systematic review of classroom retrieval practice](https://eric.ed.gov/?id=EJ1319572).

**Product implication:** Use short lessons, repeated practice, low-pressure checks, and timely review. Avoid shame-based streaks or treating missed lessons as moral failure.

### 6. Content trust and theological transparency are essential

The [Amman Message resolution on recognized schools](https://ammanmessage.com/resolution-152-islam-and-the-one-ummah-and-the-schools-of-islamic-jurisprudence/) explains that valid schools can differ in secondary rulings. The [Hadith of Gabriel](https://sunnah.com/muslim/1/5) provides a useful Islam–Iman–Ihsan structure for belief, practice, and spiritual excellence.

**Product implication:** Teach shared essentials first. Label school-specific rulings, let each mosque configure its approved Fiqh baseline, name reviewers, retain versions, and route disputed or personal questions to qualified scholars. IqamaTime must not generate AI fatwas.

## The learner journey: day 0 through year 1

### Stage 0 — Exploring Islam or preparing for Shahada

**Goal:** Allow a person to explore without pressure and understand what the Shahada means.

- Who Allah is and the purpose of life;
- the message of the prophets and Prophet Muhammad ﷺ;
- what the Shahada affirms;
- what changes immediately and what can be learned gradually;
- common misconceptions;
- private questions and optional mosque contact;
- clear consent and privacy choices;
- “I am only exploring” mode that does not assume conversion.

### Stage 1 — First 72 hours

**Goal:** Ensure the learner is not left alone and can begin worship without panic.

- welcome from the local mosque;
- named mentor or support coordinator;
- exact local prayer times and Qibla;
- visual wudu guide;
- minimum viable Salah guide;
- Al-Fatiha audio, transliteration, meaning, and repeat-after-reciter mode;
- guidance for someone still learning Arabic;
- what invalidates wudu and Salah, kept to essentials;
- a “mistakes are part of learning” reassurance;
- next local class or mosque visit;
- confidential support and safeguarding route.

**Service target:** A mentor should be assigned within 24 hours and make a first human contact within 48 hours when the learner has opted in.

### Stage 2 — Days 1–30: foundations

**Goal:** Establish confidence in belief, daily worship, and belonging.

- Allah, Tawhid, and worship;
- prophets, revealed books, angels, the Last Day, and divine decree;
- five daily prayers and a guided practice mode;
- cleanliness, ghusl, wudu, and prayer clothing;
- Quran as revelation, how to handle and approach it, and daily listening;
- Arabic letters and sounds;
- Al-Fatiha and selected short surahs;
- basic halal and haram principles without overwhelming lists;
- dhikr, dua, and Prophetic manners;
- family and workplace conversations;
- religion versus local culture;
- weekly mentor check-in.

### Stage 3 — Days 31–90: practice and identity

**Goal:** Move from assisted practice to stable routines and community connection.

- Seerah overview;
- Arabic letter joining, vowels, sukun, shaddah, and reading words;
- tajweed introduction with audio comparison;
- meanings and context of Al-Fatiha and short surahs;
- the mosque-approved beginner Fiqh path;
- Jumu’ah and mosque etiquette;
- Ramadan, Zakat, or Hajj essentials when seasonally relevant;
- food, dress, work, money, family, and social conduct;
- doubts, spiritual fluctuation, and asking for help;
- participation in a local class, gathering, or service activity.

### Stage 4 — Months 4–12: rooted learning

**Goal:** Build a durable foundation and a continuing study habit.

- deeper Aqidah with a named curriculum and reviewer;
- complete beginner Fiqh course in the mosque’s configured school;
- Quran reading fluency and tajweed practice;
- tafsir of Al-Fatiha and selected portions of Juz ‘Amma;
- Seerah in greater depth;
- curated Hadith with authenticity grading and context;
- Akhlaq, Tazkiyah, repentance, gratitude, patience, and sincerity;
- fasting, Zakat, Hajj, funerals, and other life-stage subjects;
- optional tracks for marriage, parenting, finance, and family relations;
- service, friendship, and belonging in the mosque;
- a personalized next-year study plan.

### Stage 5 — Continuing education after year 1

- structured Quran memorization and tafsir;
- Arabic vocabulary, grammar, and comprehension;
- Usul and deeper studies with qualified teachers;
- comparative Fiqh only after foundational competence;
- leadership, service, teaching-assistant, or mentor preparation.

## Curriculum tracks

The library should contain tracks, but the learner homepage should surface no more than a few relevant actions at once.

1. **Welcome and Shahada** — meaning, expectations, first contacts, privacy.
2. **Salah and purification** — wudu, ghusl, prayer movements, words, common questions.
3. **Quran relationship** — revelation, listening, meanings, daily connection.
4. **Quran literacy and tajweed** — letters through fluent guided reading.
5. **Arabic language** — vocabulary and comprehension, separate from script literacy.
6. **Aqidah** — core beliefs first; advanced and school-specific material later.
7. **Fiqh** — mosque-configured rulings with clear school and scholar metadata.
8. **Seerah** — the Prophet’s life, character, mission, and historical context.
9. **Hadith and Sunnah** — small, curated selections with grading and explanation.
10. **Akhlaq and Tazkiyah** — character, worship of the heart, habits, and spiritual health.
11. **Daily Muslim life** — home, work, food, clothing, money, relationships, and community.
12. **Islamic year** — Jumu’ah, Ramadan, Eid, Zakat, Hajj, and sacred times.
13. **Belonging and wellbeing** — family transition, isolation, culture, counseling, and referrals.
14. **Ask a scholar** — structured questions, local office hours, and escalation.

## Standard lesson design

Every lesson should be approximately 5–8 minutes and have one clear outcome.

1. **What you will learn** — one plain-language sentence.
2. **Explain** — concise approved teaching, avoiding unexplained terminology.
3. **Source** — Quran, Hadith, or named scholarly curriculum references.
4. **Show me** — diagram, animation, audio, or short video when appropriate.
5. **Practice now** — a small action the learner can complete immediately.
6. **Check understanding** — two or three low-pressure retrieval questions.
7. **Try today** — one real-life application.
8. **Ask or discuss** — a mentor prompt and scholar-escalation option.
9. **Review later** — spaced repetition date based on confidence, not punishment.

Each published version requires:

- author and qualified scholar reviewer;
- sources and school/tradition metadata;
- approved date and version;
- language and human translator;
- accessibility review;
- media rights and attribution;
- next scheduled review date.

## Personalized onboarding

Ask only questions that improve the experience, and make sensitive questions optional:

- preferred language;
- “exploring,” “new Muslim,” or “supporting someone” path;
- Quran-reading level;
- familiarity with wudu and Salah;
- preferred learning pace and format;
- local mosque and mentor preference;
- Fiqh path selected by the mosque or teacher;
- accessibility needs;
- immediate concern: prayer, family, Ramadan, loneliness, food, work, or another topic;
- communication and privacy consent.

Do not require a learner to disclose their conversion history, former religion, family conflict, immigration status, or other sensitive information. Collect the minimum religious-profile data needed and provide account deletion and export controls.

## Product experience

### Learner portal

Recommended public route: `/learn/{masjidSlug}`

The learner sees:

- **Today:** next prayer, one lesson, one practice action, and one support contact;
- **My Journey:** 72 hours, 30 days, 90 days, and year-one milestones;
- **Learn Salah:** full-screen visual practice with audio and step controls;
- **Read Quran:** verified text, translation, audio, and beginner reading mode;
- **Arabic:** letter and word practice with pronunciation;
- **Ask:** mentor message, class questions, and scholar escalation;
- **Community:** next new-Muslim class, Jumu’ah, Eid, and social event;
- **Library:** the wider approved curriculum, searchable by need;
- **Help now:** safeguarding and practical referral resources.

The homepage must never shame the learner for missed prayers or lessons. Progress language should say “continue,” “review,” and “practice,” not “failed,” “broken streak,” or “behind.”

### Mosque dashboard

Add a **New Muslims** area for authorized mosque staff:

- enable or disable the local program;
- configure public welcome information and languages;
- select the approved Fiqh path and scholar reviewers;
- register vetted mentors and availability;
- assign learners only after consent;
- schedule classes and gatherings;
- maintain local counseling, food, housing, and safety referrals;
- review aggregate participation and unanswered support requests;
- approve content and translations;
- export or delete learner data according to policy.

### Mentor workspace

- assigned learners and consented contact channel;
- first-contact and weekly check-in tasks;
- discussion prompts tied to the learner’s current module;
- learner-selected concerns and goals;
- class attendance only when the learner chooses to share it;
- scholar and safeguarding escalation;
- no access to unrelated organization administration.

### Website widgets

Offer white-label, responsive embeds for mosque websites:

- “New to Islam? Start here” welcome card;
- upcoming new-Muslim class and registration;
- Learn Salah starter card;
- mosque contact and visiting information;
- a compact “Today’s next step” widget for enrolled learners after authentication.

## Roles and permissions

| Role | Main permissions |
|---|---|
| Learner | Control profile, consent, progress, mentor contact, and data deletion |
| Mentor | View only assigned, consented learners; create check-ins; escalate concerns |
| Scholar/content reviewer | Review, approve, reject, annotate, and version religious content |
| Mosque program admin | Configure the program, cohorts, events, mentors, and approved curricula |
| Safeguarding lead | Restricted incident and referral access; independent reporting channel |
| Platform admin | Operate infrastructure without routine access to private learner notes |

## Safeguarding requirements

The [Convert Muslim Foundation safeguarding guidance](https://www.convertmuslimfoundation.org.uk/UserUpload/filemanager/source/Mosque%20safeguarding%20guidance.pdf) supports official contact channels, volunteer screening, supervised practices, confidential reporting, and named safeguarding responsibility.

Before launch:

- verify mentor identity, training, references, and mosque approval;
- keep personal phone numbers and home addresses hidden by default;
- use in-app or mosque-controlled communication;
- let learners choose mentor gender where available;
- define boundaries for one-to-one meetings and use public mosque spaces;
- provide block, reassign, and confidential report controls;
- separate safeguarding reports from ordinary mentor notes;
- log access to restricted records;
- prohibit romantic solicitation, financial requests, coercion, and unauthorized counseling;
- establish emergency, abuse, mental-health, homelessness, and domestic-violence referrals;
- build a separate guardian and safeguarding design before supporting minors.

**Recommendation:** Launch the first version for adults only. Add minors only after a dedicated legal and safeguarding review.

## Theological and editorial governance

1. Establish a named content board with qualified scholars and experienced new-Muslim educators.
2. Publish a scope statement describing the supported tradition and how valid Fiqh differences are handled.
3. Teach shared fundamentals first and delay comparative disagreements.
4. Let each mosque configure an approved school or local-teacher path.
5. Display school, reviewer, source, version, and review date on relevant lessons.
6. Never let a language model invent rulings, Quran translations, Hadith gradings, or unsourced religious answers.
7. AI search may retrieve and summarize only from approved, versioned content, with source links.
8. Personal rulings and sensitive circumstances must be escalated to a qualified human scholar.
9. Establish corrections, appeals, emergency unpublishing, and version migration procedures.
10. Include new Muslims—especially women and people from varied cultural backgrounds—in product review.

## Quran, Hadith, and media sourcing

### Quran

[Quran Foundation’s APIs](https://api-docs.quran.foundation/docs/api-reference/) provide chapters, verses, translations, tafsir, audio, and structural metadata. Their [audio documentation](https://api-docs.quran.com/docs/sdk/javascript/audio/) includes chapter/verse recitation and timing capabilities. Keep provider credentials on the server.

Rules:

- use verified Arabic Quran text without alteration;
- use approved, peer-reviewed translations;
- never machine-translate Quran text;
- retain edition, translator, reciter, and verse metadata;
- follow provider rate limits and attribution;
- support offline caching only when licensing permits it.

[Tanzil’s Quran text license](https://tanzil.net/docs/Text_License) permits verbatim distribution under its stated Creative Commons terms and requires attribution and a link; its text must not be changed.

### Hadith

- begin with a small scholar-curated collection tied to lessons;
- retain collection, book, chapter, number, language, grading, and source URL;
- provide context and avoid a random-Hadith feed for beginners;
- do not imply that one isolated narration is a complete ruling;
- verify the commercial and redistribution terms of every API before caching content.

### Third-party courses and websites

Use public pages to study curriculum patterns and link learners to authorized resources. Do not scrape and republish copyrighted lessons, videos, translations, or books. IqamaTime should either:

- license content;
- form a partner agreement;
- link to the source;
- or commission original material reviewed by its own scholars.

## Delivery plan

### Phase 0 — Governance and service design

**Indicative duration:** 2–3 weeks

- appoint scholar, new-Muslim educator, safeguarding, and product reviewers;
- agree on theological scope and mosque-level Fiqh configuration;
- approve privacy, mentor, safeguarding, and content-versioning policies;
- choose initial languages and licensing partners;
- interview new Muslims and mosque coordinators;
- finalize the first-30-days content map and service blueprint.

**Exit condition:** No product development begins on religious lessons until reviewer ownership and approval criteria are defined.

### Phase 1 — First 30 Days MVP

**Indicative duration:** 6–8 weeks after Phase 0

- learner onboarding and consent;
- first-72-hours checklist;
- local prayer time and Qibla integration;
- visual wudu and Salah path;
- Al-Fatiha audio, transliteration, meaning, and practice;
- core-belief essentials;
- mentor assignment and safe messaging;
- mosque classes and referrals;
- progress, review reminders, accessibility, and mobile support.

### Phase 2 — Quran literacy and 90-day path

**Indicative duration:** 6–8 weeks

- Arabic letters, joining, harakat, and reading practice;
- short-surah learning and tajweed introduction;
- verified Quran text, translation, and recitation APIs;
- spaced review and pronunciation exercises;
- Seerah, daily-life, and mosque-approved beginner Fiqh modules.

### Phase 3 — Mosque operations

**Indicative duration:** 4–6 weeks

- cohort and mentor dashboards;
- program configuration and content approval;
- safeguarding and referral workflows;
- multilingual content and translation review;
- aggregate analytics with privacy protections.

### Phase 4 — Public APIs and widgets

**Indicative duration:** 4–6 weeks

- welcome, class, and Learn Salah widgets;
- documented API endpoints and scoped API keys;
- white-label learner portal;
- caching, rate limits, monitoring, and accessibility testing.

### Phase 5 — Months 4–12 curriculum

- deeper Aqidah and Fiqh paths;
- tafsir, Seerah, Hadith, and character;
- Ramadan, Zakat, Hajj, finance, family, and life-stage tracks;
- continuing-study recommendations;
- partner content and teacher-led live sessions.

The durations are planning ranges, not commitments. They should be revised after Phase 0 interviews and content review.

## Success measures

### Service and belonging

- percentage of opted-in learners assigned a mentor within 24 hours;
- percentage receiving a first human contact within 48 hours;
- 7-, 30-, and 90-day belonging and safety ratings;
- attendance at one learner-selected community event;
- unresolved support, reassignment, and safeguarding requests.

### Learning and worship confidence

- ability to find local prayer time and Qibla immediately;
- self-reported wudu and Salah confidence at 7, 30, and 90 days;
- successful guided completion of a practice Salah;
- Arabic-letter, joining, and short-surah reading milestones;
- retrieval-check improvement over time;
- learner-selected goals completed, rather than raw screen time.

### Content trust

- percentage of published lessons with current scholar approval;
- translation and accessibility coverage by language;
- correction turnaround time;
- content versions awaiting review;
- API and media license compliance.

Do not use prayer completion, recitation, or lesson streaks as a public rank or moral score.

## Decisions required before implementation

1. **Audience:** Start with adults only? Recommended: yes.
2. **Scope:** Sunni multi-madhhab with mosque configuration, or a broader cross-tradition model? Recommended: shared essentials plus explicitly configured local paths.
3. **Initial languages:** Recommended: English and Arabic first; prioritize Spanish and Urdu after user research.
4. **Mentor ownership:** Will IqamaTime verify mentors centrally, or will each mosque remain accountable? Recommended: mosque accountability with platform-wide minimum standards and audit controls.
5. **Scholar ownership:** Name the scholars and approval process before content production.
6. **Learner privacy:** Recommended: learner records stay mosque-scoped, are never sold, and are not visible to other mosques without fresh consent.
7. **Content strategy:** Decide which material will be original, licensed, partnered, or linked.
8. **Live support:** Define service hours and escalation paths; never imply 24/7 support unless it exists.

## Recommended immediate next action

Run a two-week discovery and governance sprint before writing production code:

1. Interview 8–12 new Muslims at different stages, including women and people with limited mosque access.
2. Interview 4–6 new-Muslim coordinators, mentors, and imams.
3. Convene one curriculum workshop and one safeguarding workshop.
4. Approve the first-72-hours and first-30-days content maps.
5. Prototype the learner “Today” page, Learn Salah flow, and mentor check-in.
6. Test those prototypes with at least five learners.
7. Only then finalize the database and API contracts for Phase 1.

This approach gives IqamaTime a genuine support system rather than another uncurated Islamic content library.
